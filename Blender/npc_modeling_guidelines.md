# The Living Canvas — NPC Modeling Guidelines

## Purpose

NPCs should feel like stylized people inhabiting the same painterly world as the museum.

The desired language is:

- low-to-mid poly
- expressive
- readable
- stylized
- handcrafted
- painterly through materials/textures

---

## 1. Character Style

Characters should NOT be:

- generic Roblox-like low-poly humans
- faceless mannequins
- hyper-realistic humans
- extreme chibi characters
- overly cartoonish unless specifically requested

Target:

> believable stylized human with expressive facial structure.

---

## 2. Proportions

Use believable human proportions with moderate stylization.

Approximate human scale should remain consistent with the existing museum.

Avoid:

- giant heads
- tiny bodies
- extremely short limbs
- exaggerated anatomy

Unless the specific character design requires it.

---

## 3. Face

Facial structure must remain readable.

Prioritize:

- nose
- jaw
- cheeks
- eyes
- eyebrows
- mouth
- ears

The face does not need realistic anatomy.

It needs expression.

---

## 4. NPC Silhouette

Before detailing the face, the character should already be recognizable from:

- body shape
- hairstyle
- clothing silhouette
- posture

Silhouette is especially important for first-person encounters.

---

## 5. Hair

Use stylized hair masses.

Prefer:

- large clumps
- layered shapes
- intentional silhouettes
- controlled irregularity

Avoid:

- individual strand modeling
- hair simulation
- overly glossy realistic hair

---

## 6. Clothing

Clothing should use simplified but recognizable forms.

Model:

- collars
- sleeves
- cuffs
- coats
- shoes
- bags
- hats
- other character-defining elements

Do not model microscopic stitching unless specifically needed.

---

## 7. Expressions

NPC expressions should remain readable without requiring high polygon density.

Use geometry where it improves:

- silhouette
- facial planes
- expression

Use textures/materials where appropriate for:

- subtle skin variation
- painterly color
- small surface information

---

## 8. Receptionist Example

The museum receptionist is a good reference for the project's NPC language.

The desired style is similar to the previously approved direction:

- dark hair
- attractive but natural face
- visible nose and facial structure
- subtle stylization
- expressive eyes
- low-to-mid poly construction
- painterly textures
- not photorealistic

This is a style reference, not a rigid character specification for every NPC.

---

## 9. Rigging Considerations

Model with deformation in mind.

Avoid geometry that creates obvious problems around:

- shoulders
- elbows
- knees
- neck
- jaw

Keep topology sufficient for animation without overbuilding.

---

## 10. NPC Workflow

### Phase 1
Block out body proportions.

### Phase 2
Establish head and facial planes.

### Phase 3
Establish hair silhouette.

### Phase 4
Create clothing masses.

### Phase 5
Add facial/expression-supporting geometry.

### Phase 6
Clean topology and transforms.

### Phase 7
Prepare for Unity.

---

## 11. Character Consistency

Before creating a new NPC, inspect existing NPCs.

Maintain consistency in:

- head proportions
- eye placement
- polygon density
- body scale
- stylization
- material language

Characters can have different personalities without looking like they came from different games.

---

## 12. Most Important Rule

Characters should communicate personality through:

**silhouette + face + posture + clothing**

rather than through excessive geometry.
