# The Living Canvas — Environment & Scene Integration Guidelines

## Scope

This document governs **new environment elements and scene dressing**.

IMPORTANT:

The museum interior and exterior are already modeled.

Do not rebuild or redesign them unless explicitly requested.

The job of new modeling is to make the existing museum feel complete, lived-in, coherent, and artistically consistent.

---

## 1. Existing Museum Is the Source of Truth

Before adding environment assets:

1. inspect the current museum
2. understand its scale
3. inspect its architectural language
4. identify existing materials
5. identify reusable modules
6. identify areas that need dressing

Do not assume a new architecture is needed simply because something is missing.

---

## 2. Environment Goals

The museum should feel:

- cozy
- slightly old
- cared for
- believable
- quiet
- intimate
- artistic

It should not feel:

- abandoned everywhere
- excessively luxurious
- sterile
- modern corporate
- fantasy castle
- generic game museum

The environment should support the protagonist's emotional journey.

---

## 3. Scene Dressing

Prioritize objects that communicate:

- museum function
- history
- maintenance
- personality
- daily life
- passage of time

Examples:

- cleaning equipment
- maintenance supplies
- visitor signs
- small benches
- display supports
- lamps
- plants
- framed artworks
- storage elements
- staff objects
- subtle clutter

Do not fill every empty space.

Negative space is important for the contemplative tone.

---

## 4. Museum Rooms

Do not redesign existing rooms.

Instead, improve their identity through dressing.

Each room should have:

- a clear visual purpose
- a controlled amount of decoration
- a consistent palette
- a few memorable focal elements

Avoid making every room equally decorated.

---

## 5. Maintenance Areas

Maintenance spaces may be:

- less polished
- more functional
- slightly cluttered
- visually warmer or more worn

But they should still belong to the same world.

Useful elements:

- cleaning cart
- buckets
- mops
- shelves
- tools
- replacement bulbs
- boxes
- maintenance signage
- old equipment

---

## 6. Basement

If the existing basement is used or expanded, it can become visually more neglected than public museum areas.

Use:

- older materials
- darker atmosphere
- irregular storage
- exposed practical elements
- forgotten objects

Do not automatically turn it into horror.

The Living Canvas is primarily cozy and contemplative.

Unease should be subtle and purposeful.

---

## 7. Exterior

The exterior already exists.

New exterior assets should reinforce:

- approachability
- quietness
- small-museum identity
- surrounding atmosphere
- handcrafted painterly character

Use environmental dressing rather than architectural reconstruction.

---

## 8. Vegetation

Vegetation should feel painterly rather than botanically perfect.

Prefer:

- readable masses
- varied silhouettes
- controlled asymmetry
- hand-authored placement

Avoid repetitive identical vegetation.

---

## 9. Lighting Support

Modeling should support lighting.

Consider:

- windows
- lamps
- architectural openings
- display cases
- reflective surfaces
- shadow-producing objects

Do not add geometry solely to imitate lighting that should be handled by Unity.

---

## 10. Performance

Use detail strategically.

Background objects can be simpler.

Hero areas deserve more detail.

Avoid unnecessary unique meshes when reusable assets would work.

---

## 11. First-Person Composition

Always consider what the player sees while walking.

Important objects should create:

- visual landmarks
- natural points of interest
- framing
- depth
- comfortable navigation

Avoid placing decorative objects directly in the player's path unless intentional.

---

## 12. Final Environment Rule

The goal is not to make the museum more complicated.

The goal is to make the existing museum feel more **intentional, alive, and coherent**.
